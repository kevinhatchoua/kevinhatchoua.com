## Client

Massachusetts' Human Resources Division (HRD) is responsible for attracting, hiring and developing people to do purposeful work. We partner with agencies to build and support a high-performing diverse workforce.

## Project

HRD, in collaboration with the Governor's office, is building a public-facing dashboard with information on state employee demographics to be more transparent about gender and race diversity in its workforce.

## Challenges

There has been critique of the general dashboard aesthetic that requires our attention, namely, that the dashboard is not as attention-grabbing as it could be. HRD has asked us to make design suggestions, which meet accessibility requirements, that address this critique.

## Tools & technology

- Tableau
- Illustrator
- Photoshop
- JavaScript

## Solution

We explored a more engaging presentation while keeping the data honest and readable: stronger color contrast for gender and race breakdowns, a clearer page frame with filters and headline metrics, geographic context on a Massachusetts map, and chart types that draw the eye without obscuring the numbers.

### Before

Earlier Tableau views used muted greens and grays across separate race, gender, and combined charts. The information was accurate but easy to scan past.

![Before: workforce by race and gender Tableau charts for 2020 Q1](/images/work/hrd/dashboard-before.png)

*Before: default chart styling and separated views.*

### Final version

The proposed **Employee Demographics – Executive Branch** layout groups quarterly trends, county distribution, race and gender breakdowns, and an overall gender split into one dashboard with consistent navy and cyan accents, filter controls, and a single narrative for transparency.

![Final version: Employee Demographics executive branch dashboard](/images/work/hrd/dashboard-final.png)

*Final version: unified dashboard with filters, map, and accessible color system.*

[View the live State Employee Diversity Dashboard](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/state-employee-diversity-dashboard)

## Reflection

When data is about people, tone matters as much as typography. Attention-grabbing should mean easier to understand, not louder for its own sake.
