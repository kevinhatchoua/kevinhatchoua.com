In UX design, we sometimes get stuck in the realm of belief when we're really facing an engineering challenge.

![Icon of idea and engineering](/images/blog/belief-and-engineering.png)

Seth Godin offers a great metaphor for this: "If you can build a bridge out of pasta, show me!" This playful challenge encapsulates the essence of engineering. It's not about what we believe should work; it's about what actually works.

Too often, we cling to beliefs about users:

- "Our customers want simplicity."
- "Dark mode is always preferred by tech-savvy users."
- "This feature will drive engagement."

These statements may feel true, but without evidence, they're merely assumptions that can be costly.

In contrast, engineering is about seeking to be proven wrong. It's an iterative process of improvement where every failure is celebrated as a step closer to the truth. It helps us reframe failure as an opportunity to ask, "Why didn't this work, and how can we make it better?"

Adopting this mindset means:

- Testing designs, even when you're confident they'll work.
- Actively searching for friction points and blind spots.
- Viewing user feedback not as a critique but as a gift.

Belief drives innovations, but engineering drives certainty. This is how we make things better, by making better things.
