We are used to building for the masses, the standard, and the average.

There is nothing wrong with this idea, if your goal is to build an average product or deliver average work.

![Minimum viable audience diagram](/images/blog/smallest-viable-audience.png)

It's an evolutionary trait we've honed and refined over millennia, and justly so. It's kept us safe and it's kept us fed. This is all thanks to the amygdala. An almond-sized region of our brain that does not speak English yet is powerful enough to protect us from harm, criticism, and failure.

It also happens to be the primary reason for our neophobia.

The fear of the new, the fear of the unknown.

So we play it safe and build average products to please everyone. Something just average enough, to keep us from what we fear most, rejection.

The truth is no one is average.

Long before my time, cars were made in a standard way. Every jigsaw piece had to fit ever so perfectly. Disrupting this system meant disrupting the assembly line, which in turn meant disrupting production and revenue. Getting a custom piece is out of the question, it cost too much. So car manufacturers did what they do best, and standardize all parts.

While it was more efficient, it did not make for better cars. In fact, what they discovered was they were building average cars for "average" people.

Too tall or too short? You are out of luck!

Realizing this, Ford pioneered a four-way seat adjustment mechanism that would account for various body shapes and sizes.

The range of motion was simple. Fore/aft and up/down. You guessed it, their customers loved it! Decades later it's the new "average".

If you are passionate about your work, if you care about the things you build, you have to focus on the smallest viable audience.

The masses aren't interested in what you are selling, and you can't make them, but that's ok!

Knowing this allows us to focus our full attention on those that matter. Those that are willing to dance with you. Embrace and invest in your ideas.

"The smallest viable audience approach means figuring out who the people are who care enough about what you're selling to get in their car and drive five miles, or twenty miles, instead of walking across the street to buy something average." ([Seth Godin](https://seths.blog/))
